Lentils

Lentils are one of the most practical foods for building an anti-inflammatory meal pattern because they are affordable, filling, and easy to use in soups, bowls, and meal prep.

Quick answer

Lentils fit well into anti-inflammatory eating because they are a practical whole-food staple with fiber and plant protein. Their main advantage is that they help create repeatable, simple meals.

What it is

Lentils are edible legumes commonly used in soups, stews, bowls, and simple batch-cooked meals.

Why lentils may support an anti-inflammatory diet

Lentils are useful because they create a dependable meal base that supports fiber-rich, plant-forward eating without much complexity.

Key nutrients and compounds

  • Fiber
  • Plant protein
  • Folate
  • Iron

Potential health benefits

  • Supports affordable whole-food meals
  • Adds structure to soups and grain bowls
  • Works well in meal prep

How to eat lentils

  • Cook lentil soup or stew
  • Add lentils to grain bowls
  • Use lentils in simple batch-cooked lunches

Shopping and storage

Store dry lentils in a cool, dry place. Cooked lentils should be refrigerated and used within a few days.

FAQ

Are lentils anti-inflammatory?

They are commonly included in anti-inflammatory food patterns because they are a practical whole-food staple with fiber and plant protein.

Evidence note

Lentils are described here as a supportive staple within a broader eating pattern, not as a treatment by themselves.

The strongest factual framing on this page is limited to established nutrition context such as fiber, plant protein, folate, and iron. The page does not claim that eating lentils guarantees a specific health outcome.
