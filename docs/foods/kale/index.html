Kale

Kale is a leafy green that broadens the vegetable cluster with another practical meal ingredient. It fits anti-inflammatory meal patterns best when used in repeatable salads and cooked dishes.

Quick answer

Kale is often included in anti-inflammatory food patterns because it is a leafy green that adds variety and works in both cooked meals and salads.

What it is

Kale is a leafy green vegetable commonly used in salads, sautés, soups, and baked dishes.

Why kale may support an anti-inflammatory diet

Kale helps extend leafy green intake and creates more variety in the vegetable category without changing the basic meal pattern.

Key nutrients and compounds

  • Vitamin K
  • Vitamin C
  • Fiber
  • Carotenoids

Potential health benefits

  • Expands leafy green variety
  • Works in salads and cooked dishes
  • Supports vegetable-forward meals

How to eat kale

  • Massage kale for salads
  • Sauté it with garlic and olive oil
  • Add it to soups and grain bowls

Shopping and storage

Choose crisp leaves and refrigerate them well. Use within several days for best texture.

FAQ

Is kale anti-inflammatory?

It is commonly included in anti-inflammatory food patterns as a leafy green that supports vegetable variety.

Evidence note

Kale is described here as a supportive leafy green within a broader eating pattern, not as a treatment by itself.

The strongest factual framing on this page is limited to established nutrition context such as vitamin K, vitamin C, fiber, and carotenoids. The page does not claim that eating kale guarantees a specific health outcome.
